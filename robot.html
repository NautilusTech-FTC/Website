Overview

Our robot design utilizes a rubber band roller in the front to intake balls up a ramp into the robot. The balls are then stored along a curved wall to maximize space and store up to 3 balls. A “spoon” under the ball in the shooter chamber then lifts it into our shooter, which is able to consistently shoot balls into the goals from the near and far launch zone.

Transfer

Our robot features a curved wall to transport the balls from the intake to the shooter. This design was created to maximize the space available on the robot. We found that storing the balls flat would take up too much space, so curving it allowed us to use the middle for other components. The curved design also enabled us to move all the balls at once using a single servo with flexible flaps. We also implemented a removable wall, the wall completely detaches from the base, giving us easy access to some wiring.

Sensors, Camera, & Lights

Whether it be for shooting, aiming, intaking or moving, we use sensors for everything.

For aiming, we used our own localizer, which evolved over the course of the season. Initially starting out as purely camera input, we've slowly started evolving more data sources into it, starting with the IMU, and at the end we used Pinpoint to locate our robot during tele-op. This system has fail safes, so if we get knocked and our dead-wheels lose track, we can just use our camera to recalibrate them and get right back to aiming perfectly. We then take this IMU data and do some computations with it, so that we can calculate the perfect heading while still accounting for our offset shooter.

We also have other sensors that are used during the game, like a color sensor, that just toggles one of our lights to light up in the color of the ball that's currently in the shooter. This allows the drivers to quickly tell what ball we are about to shoot.

One of our most important sensors though, is the encoder of our shooter motor. This allows us to use PIDF to shoot with accuracy every single time. Without this, our shots would be inconsistent, and take forever to get back up to speed.
